II. Abstract Addressing the intelligent collaborative interference decision problem of frequency jitter and frequency diversity in cognitive electronic warfare, this paper proposes an intelligent collaborative interference strategy decision method based on hierarchical multi-agent reinforcement learning. This method utilizes a multi-agent Markov decision process to construct a multi-interference machine collaborative decision process, establishes a frequency domain collaborative interference decision model, and introduces the design concept of hierarchical reinforcement learning. To solve for the optimal strategy, a dual deep Q network (PER-DDQN) optimization method based on the experience replay structure with tree priority is adopted. The performance of the frequency domain collaborative interference decision model based on PER-DDQN is verified through simulation. The results show that the proposed PER-DDQN method significantly outperforms the deep Q network (DQN) method in action estimation and converges faster than the dual deep Q network (DDQN) method. Furthermore, the intelligent decision method for collaborative interference proposed in this paper can formulate frequency domain parameter decision strategies according to the real-time detection of radar threat levels, effectively achieving the design objective of frequency domain intelligent decision.
III. Introduction With the rapid development of electronic countermeasures and defense technology, the integration of artificial intelligence into cognitive electronic warfare is gradually moving towards intelligent and networked directions, becoming the main combat form in modern warfare. Its core encompasses two major areas: cognitive radar countermeasures and cognitive communication countermeasures, focusing on autonomous interactive environment learning and dynamic intelligent countermeasures. Intelligent interference decision-making is a core component of the cognitive countermeasure system, capable of dynamically formulating optimal interference strategies based on battlefield situations, effectively enhancing the success rate of interference under limited interference resources.
Currently, the field of academia predominantly employs reinforcement learning-related methods to study intelligent interference decision-making technology. This can provide rapid and accurate auxiliary decisions for commanders, even applicable to unmanned aerial vehicle combat platforms. Compared with traditional interference decision-making methods based on template matching, game theory, and inference, reinforcement learning does not require prior knowledge. The agent can achieve maximization of rewards through interaction with the environment, further enhancing the method's flexibility and real-time capabilities. This makes it more suitable for addressing multidimensional, large-scale, small sample decision-making problems in battlefield environments.
Existing research on cognitive interference decision-making based on reinforcement learning primarily focuses on the changes in the enemy radar's operational status. Starting from the interference mode, by establishing a model of the radar and the interferer's confrontation, optimal strategies are sought. However, such studies only concentrate on physical layer modes, failing to fully leverage the advantages of intelligent interference from multiple dimensions of comprehensive decision-making. The frequency domain, as an important signal feature area, is a crucial entry point for radar interference waveform design and a key foothold for interference combat. The emergence of technologies such as frequency hopping and frequency diversity in radar anti-interference has made traditional narrowband speckle interference, deception forwarding interference difficult to quickly track, and broadband blocking interference also less effective due to reduced power spectral density of the interference. How to optimize the allocation of interference resources to achieve optimal interference effects with fewer interference forces, while not occupying excessive electromagnetic spectrum resources and saving interference signal transmission power, has become an urgent problem. Therefore, research on resource decision-making for intelligent interference in the frequency domain under frequency domain radar anti-interference measures is of great significance.
In battlefield environments, intelligent decision-making in frequency domain parameters faces the challenges of multiple targets and complex and variable frequency distributions, leading to exponentially increasing numbers of solutions that are unmanageable. Traditional reinforcement learning relies on querying a Q-table for decisions, significantly reducing decision efficiency in multi-objective scenarios and failing to meet the needs of actual battlefield environments. Deep reinforcement learning, bypassing or requiring minimal prior knowledge, learns optimal decision outcomes through interaction with the environment, utilizing neural networks to fit output action plans. This approach becomes even more efficient when interference actions increase and the number of actions rises.
In current research, scholars have applied the concepts of hierarchical deep reinforcement learning and multi-agent reinforcement learning to issues such as interference frequency decision-making, interference resource allocation, and interference channel access. These methods have been validated for their effectiveness in resource allocation and interference decision-making, providing a framework for integrating hierarchical reinforcement learning with multi-agent reinforcement learning for application in frequency domain collaborative interference decision-making. Building on this foundation, this paper explores the development of a multi-interference machine intelligent decision-making method for frequency domains, addressing the challenges posed by frequency jitter and diversity.
IV. Method Overview This paper focuses on the construction of the Frequency Domain Cooperative Interference Decision (FD-CJDM) model and the design of the PER-DDQN optimization algorithm. It begins by modeling the basic algorithms of DQN and DDQN, as well as adversarial scenarios. The overall method system is divided into four parts: foundational algorithm preparation, adversarial scenario construction, FD-CJDM model establishment, and training optimization module design.
1  
Basic reinforcement learning algorithm
•  
DQN: A typical representative of deep reinforcement learning, it approximates the behavior value (Q-value) under a given state through neural networks. It follows a greedy strategy to select actions and employs a target network and experience replay mechanism to address training instability and overfitting issues. The target network delays updating to generate target Q-values, while experience replay randomly samples data to reduce sample correlation.
•  
DDQN: To address the issue of overly optimistic action estimation in DQN using max operator, this paper proposes a method to decouple action Q-values through alternating updates of two networks. The action selection and Q-value computation are completed by two networks respectively, effectively suppressing the phenomenon of overestimation and enhancing the stability of the algorithm.
2  
Building a combat scenario
Constructing a typical scenario of multi-to-multi radar cognitive countermeasures, our warning aircraft commands fighter jets equipped with jamming pods to execute penetration missions. Upon detecting the target, the enemy's networked radar will employ frequency jittering and diversity reception techniques to switch to frequencies devoid of or weak interference. Simultaneously, it is specified that the networked radar adopts an even distribution of stations, with no primary defensive direction, thereby forming a comprehensive air defense force through full-frequency coverage. Additionally, the scenario assumes uniform spectrum components within the bandwidth, identical power output from each jamming pod, and precise detection of the enemy's frequency planning by us, enabling signal separation and interference scheme planning. A power criterion for the jamming frequency jittering radar is provided; interference is deemed effective when the dBSR (dry signal to noise ratio) exceeds the suppression coefficient.
1  
FD-CJDM model establishment
•  
Markov Decision Process with Centralized Learning Paradigm: Consider all disturbance buoys as homogeneous intelligent agents, constructing a fully cooperative multi-agent reinforcement learning task. By treating all agents as a single entity for learning, the integration of all agent information facilitates the resolution of global optimal solutions. This process is formally represented in the form of a quintuple.
•  
Hierarchical Reinforcement Learning Task Decomposition: To address the dimensionality disaster caused by exponential growth in action and state spaces due to multi-agent interactions, this paper employs a hierarchical reinforcement learning problem decomposition approach. It breaks down the global frequency domain interference task (FC) into its highest-level "attack target (AT)" subtasks, and further decomposes these ATs based on their threat levels into interference frequency bands (FB) and bandwidths (BW) subtasks. Agents execute each sub-strategy sequentially according to a timeline, with rewards from each sub-task accumulating and feedbacking back to the highest-level task until training is complete.
•  
Model State, Action, and Reward Space Design: The decision-making layer is divided into two types of decision-makers: one for interference frequency band decisions and another for interference bandwidth decisions. Each type of decision-maker is designed with corresponding state spaces, including the number of sub-band frequency points, threats, and bandwidth parameters among other core information; the action space is the interference sub-band selection output by the decision-maker and the bandwidth selection from the preset bandwidth library; the reward space is designed separately for both decision-makers to implement multi-dimensional reward functions. These include introducing metrics such as threat coefficients, frequency point coverage, and redundant bandwidth, guiding the agent to make optimal decisions through positive and negative rewards, achieving the goal of prioritizing high-threat targets and saving interference resources.
2  
Training and Optimization Module Design:
This module is the core of the PER-DDQN algorithm, comprising two parts: Prior Experience Replay (PER) and Importance Sampling Techniques:
•  
Prior Experience Replay: Abandoning the traditional uniform experience sampling method, using time difference error (TD-error) as the criterion for measuring sample importance, assigning priorities proportional to TD-error to samples, ensuring that important samples are sampled more frequently. This approach also introduces a very small value to avoid sampling probability zero, enhancing network training efficiency.
•  
Importance Sampling Techniques: Using an and tree structure to store experience data, dividing nodes into leaf nodes, branch nodes, and root nodes. Leaf nodes store samples and their priorities, while branch and root nodes store sums of priorities of their child nodes, reducing the data complexity brought about by large computations. To address issues of insufficient diversity and overfitting caused by TD-error priority, weighted TD-error is introduced, along with importance weights and normalized processing, minimizing gradients and parameter update steps, achieving unbiased updates.
5. Conclusion This paper aims to study the problem of interference allocation under limited interference resource constraints, prioritizing interference against high-threat targets with as few interference resources as possible. It proposes a collaborative interference intelligent decision-making method based on hierarchical multi-agent reinforcement learning. The method constructs a frequency domain collaborative interference decision (FD-CJDM) model based on a centralized learning paradigm for multi-agent Markov decision processes, effectively solving the dimensionality disaster issue in decision systems through the concept of hierarchical reinforcement learning; meanwhile, it adopts and tree structure optimization of PER-DDQN algorithm, significantly enhancing the convergence performance of the algorithm.
Theoretically speaking, the interference intelligent decision-making method proposed in this paper has strong applicability, which can be applied almost to all interference intelligent agents' frequency band and bandwidth parameter resource allocation issues, whether for single resource allocation or centralized multi-resource allocation, for single radar systems or multiple target radar network scenarios. The method optimizes to avoid complex large action space issues, providing a new effective solution for interference resource decision in cognitive electronic warfare.
